# 🗼 Tokyo Travel Plan

A comprehensive guide for visiting Tokyo, Japan.

---

## 📅 Suggested Itinerary: 7 Days in Tokyo

### Day 1 – Arrival & Shinjuku
- Arrive at **Narita International Airport** or **Haneda Airport**
- Take the Narita Express (N'EX) or Limousine Bus to central Tokyo
- Check in to your hotel in **Shinjuku**
- Evening: Explore **Shinjuku Kabukicho** (entertainment district) and **Omoide Yokocho** (Memory Lane) for yakitori and ramen
- Visit **Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building** for free night views of the city

### Day 2 – Asakusa & Akihabara
- Morning: **Senso-ji Temple** (Tokyo's oldest temple) in Asakusa
- Stroll through **Nakamise Shopping Street** for souvenirs and traditional snacks
- Afternoon: Head to **Akihabara** – the electronics and anime/manga hub
- Evening: Try authentic sushi at **Tsukiji Outer Market** nearby

### Day 3 – Harajuku, Shibuya & Omotesando
- Morning: **Meiji Jingu Shrine** – a tranquil forested shrine in the heart of the city
- **Harajuku's Takeshita Street** – quirky fashion and crepes
- Afternoon: **Shibuya Crossing** – the world's busiest pedestrian intersection
- Visit **Shibuya Sky** observation deck for panoramic views
- Evening: Upscale shopping at **Omotesando Hills**

### Day 4 – Odaiba & TeamLab
- Morning: Take the **Yurikamome Line** to Odaiba (futuristic waterfront district)
- Visit **teamLab Borderless** or **teamLab Planets** (digital art museums – book in advance!)
- **Odaiba Seaside Park** with views of Rainbow Bridge and the replica Statue of Liberty
- Afternoon: **DiverCity Tokyo Plaza** for Gundam statue and shopping
- Evening: Dinner with Tokyo Bay views

### Day 5 – Ueno, Yanaka & Ikebukuro
- Morning: **Ueno Park** – visit the zoo, Tokyo National Museum, and cherry blossom paths
- Explore **Yanaka Ginza** – a preserved old-Tokyo shopping street
- Afternoon: **Ikebukuro** – Sunshine City aquarium and shopping
- Evening: **Sunshine 60** observation deck

### Day 6 – Day Trip: Nikko or Kamakura
**Option A – Nikko** (2.5 hrs by train):
  - **Tosho-gu Shrine** – elaborate mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu
  - Scenic waterfalls and mountain landscapes

**Option B – Kamakura** (1 hr by train):
  - **Kotoku-in Giant Buddha** (Daibutsu)
  - **Hase-dera Temple** with sea views
  - Coastal town with great seafood

### Day 7 – Ginza, Tsukiji & Departure
- Morning: **Tsukiji Outer Market** – fresh sushi breakfast (the real foodie experience)
- **Ginza** – Tokyo's upscale shopping district (flagship stores of major brands)
- **teamLab** (if not visited yet) or relax at a **onsen** (hot spring spa)
- Head to airport for departure

## 🍜 Must-Try Foods

- **Ramen** – Try different regional styles (Tonkotsu, Shoyu, Miso)
- **Sushi & Sashimi** – Tsukiji and Ginza have top-tier options
- **Tempura** – Light, crispy battered seafood and vegetables
- **Yakitori** – Grilled chicken skewers (best in Yurakucho under the tracks)
- **Wagyu Beef** – Japanese A5 beef, a splurge worth making
- **Takoyaki** – Octopus balls (a popular street snack)
- **Matcha desserts** – Matcha ice cream, parfaits, and lattes
- **Conveyor belt sushi (Kaiten-zushi)** – Fun and affordable

---

## 🚇 Getting Around

- **IC Card (Suica or Pasmo)** – Load money and tap to ride any train, subway, or bus. Also works at convenience stores.
- **Tokyo Metro Day Pass** – Great value if you plan multiple subway trips per day (~¥800–¥1,500)
- **JR Pass** – Useful if doing day trips (Nikko, Kamakura, Kyoto)
- **Taxis** – Convenient but expensive; use for late-night travel
- **Cycling** – Many areas have bike-share programs (Docomo Bike Share)

## 🌸 Best Time to Visit

| Season | Months | Highlights |
|--------|--------|------------|
| Spring | March–May | **Cherry Blossoms (Sakura)** – most popular season |
| Summer | June–August | Festivals (Matsuri), fireworks; hot & humid |
| Autumn | September–November | Fall foliage; comfortable temperatures |
| Winter | December–February | Illuminations, fewer crowds, occasional snow |

> **Best overall:** Late March–April (sakura season) or October–November (autumn leaves)

## 📱 Useful Apps & Tips

- **Google Maps** – Works great for Tokyo transit navigation
- **Japan Official Travel App** – Tourism information
- **Tabelog / OpenRice** – Restaurant reviews and reservations
- **PayPay / IC Card** – Digital payments; Japan is still largely cash-based, carry yen
- **Pocket WiFi or SIM Card** – Rent at the airport for reliable internet
- **7-Eleven / FamilyMart / Lawson** – Convenience stores are your best friends (cheap, quality food 24/7)

---

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- **Language**: English signage is common at major attractions and stations; Google Translate camera mode is handy
- **Etiquette**: Be quiet on trains, don't eat while walking, remove shoes when required
- **Tipping**: Not customary in Japan; may even be considered rude
- **Cash**: Many smaller restaurants and shops are cash-only; keep ¥10,000–¥20,000 on hand
- **Trash cans**: Very rare in public; carry a small bag for your trash
- **Emergency**: Dial **110** (Police) or **119** (Fire/Ambulance)
